## Executive Summary

This framework separates the current empirical comparison of five underground and five opencast quadrats from a proposed future restoration-monitoring program. The present study contains three-season soil, vegetation, ground-cover, and satellite-derived observations from the sampled mine quadrats. These observations suggest higher soil carbon and nitrogen in the underground group, greater heterogeneity among opencast quadrats, and stronger seasonal vegetation turnover at some opencast locations. The proposed second-phase design would add unmined reference sites, independent site replication, restoration-history data, soil physical and biological indicators, quantitative vegetation structure, fauna, and multi-year remote sensing. These additions are methodological recommendations and are not treated as completed measurements.

## Part I: Current Empirical Evidence (Baseline & Scope)

### 1.1 Existing Empirical Dataset Summary
The present investigation is anchored upon 10 permanently marked $10 \times 10\text{ m}$ quadrats established in the Korba coalfield, Chhattisgarh, India (*Sharma, Banerjee & Deb, 2026*):
- **5 Underground Mining Lease Quadrats (UG-Q1 to UG-Q5):** Located in Banki, Balgi, Surakachhar, and Dhelwadih colliery sectors (elevations 283.9–315.3 m). Surface soils and secondary tree cover remain largely intact above room-and-pillar workings.
- **5 Opencast Mining Project Quadrats (OC-Q1 to OC-Q5):** Located in the Gevra and Dipka opencast mine complexes (elevations 298.5–310.2 m). Situated across technical reclamation plantations, sump drainage borders, rocky overburden spoil, and disturbed perimeter berms.

### 1.2 Audited Empirical Findings
1. **Soil Carbon and Fertility:** Underground quadrats consistently exhibit higher soil organic carbon ($1.28–1.56\%$ SoilGrids; $0.45–0.65\%$ measured 0–15 cm) and available nitrogen ($174.2–335.5\text{ kg/ha}$) compared to raw opencast spoil ($93.5–256.2\text{ kg/ha}$).
2. **Woody Stem Census:** Exactly 58 individual tree and woody subcanopy stems ($\text{GBH} \ge 10\text{ cm}$) across 24 accepted species (representing 25 reported field morphotaxa) were directly measured. Tree carbon stocks were significantly higher in underground quadrats (median $24.16\text{ t C/ha}$) than in opencast quadrats (median $16.81\text{ t C/ha}$; Mann-Whitney $U = 0.0$, $p = 0.0079$).
3. **Seasonal Understory Dynamics:** Across Summer, Monsoon, and Winter, 42 regional plant species were recorded across strata. Opencast sites exhibited greater structural heterogeneity, ranging from persistent woody shrub cover to pronounced seasonal turnover driven by annual forbs.
4. **Audited Six-Date Sentinel-2 Observations:** Screened under strict terrestrial Scene Classification ($\text{SCL} \in \{4, 5\}$), underground sites displayed stable winter-to-monsoon NDVI ratios ($0.84–1.11$), whereas open disturbed spoil exhibited high seasonal amplitude ($\Delta\text{NDVI}$ up to $0.2854$) due to transient monsoon weed flushes.

### 1.3 Core Methodological Limitations
The existing dataset is bounded by four key constraints:
- **Small Sample Size:** $n=5$ per group represents an exploratory pilot comparison.
- **No Reference Baseline:** Absence of unmined forest controls prevents calculating absolute recovery.
- **Spatial Clustering:** Quadrats are clustered within two colliery complexes without broader spatial replication.
- **Satellite Temporal Resolution:** Six discrete dates provide seasonal snapshots rather than continuous phenological curves.

### 2.3 Proposed Mechanistic Comparison: Testing Divergences Between OC-Q4 and OC-Q5
A major pattern in the empirical dataset is the divergence between two opencast quadrats:
- **Observed Reality in OC-Q4:** Supports a persistent *Holarrhena pubescens* thicket (5 measured stems $\ge 10\text{ cm}$ GBH), maintaining high winter greenness (Winter NDVI = 0.6703; Winter-to-Monsoon ratio = 1.0055).
- **Observed Reality in OC-Q5:** Dominated by bare ground and coarse fragments, displaying low summer NDVI ($0.2137$), an intense monsoon weed flush ($0.4992$) associated with *Parthenium* and *Senna*, and the highest seasonal amplitude ($\Delta\text{NDVI} = 0.2854$).

#### Candidate Mechanisms to Test in Future Research (Hypotheses, Not Completed Findings):
1. **Soil Compaction Hypothesis:** OC-Q5 may experience mechanical penetration resistance exceeding the critical root impedance threshold ($> 2.5\text{ MPa}$), restricting root systems to a shallow surface layer ($< 10\text{ cm}$), whereas OC-Q4 possesses a deeper, less compacted rooting zone.
2. **Hydraulic Infiltration Hypothesis:** Surface crusting and low macro-porosity on raw spoil in OC-Q5 may restrict water infiltration to $< 5\text{ mm/h}$, inducing rapid runoff, whereas OC-Q4 maintains higher percolation ($> 15\text{ mm/h}$).
3. **Topsoil Replacement & Age Hypothesis:** OC-Q4's stability may stem from earlier topsoil capping ($10–20\text{ cm}$) and older substrate age ($> 10\text{ years}$), whereas OC-Q5 represents younger, unamended overburden.
4. **Seed Source Distance Hypothesis:** Proximity to intact forest patches may govern the arrival of native woody propagules (*Holarrhena*), whereas isolated spoil relies entirely on airborne ruderal weed seeds (*Parthenium*).

## Part III: Prospective Study Design (Protocols & Sampling Architecture)

### 3.1 Undisturbed Reference-Site Selection Protocol
To evaluate ecological recovery relative to an unmined benchmark, a future campaign should establish permanent reference quadrats in the Katghora and Lemru Reserve Forests. 

#### Mandatory Pre-Sampling Verification Checklist:
- [ ] **Land-Use History Verification:** Verify from State Forest Department records that the candidate compartment has experienced zero mining, quarrying, or commercial logging for $\ge 50\text{ years}$.
- [ ] **Soil Parent Material Documentation:** Confirm Barakar/Raniganj sandstone-shale weathering matching the mine concession geology.
- [ ] **Topographic Equivalence:** Restrict candidate sites to elevations of $290–330\text{ m}$, slopes of $2–5^\circ$, and comparable drainage positions.
- [ ] **Absence of Severe Disturbance:** Inspect for absence of recent forest fires, heavy livestock corrals, or major road corridors.
- [ ] **Protected-Area Compliance:** Obtain formal research entry permits from the Chhattisgarh State Forest Department.
- [ ] **Independent GPS Survey:** Record 4-corner plot boundaries with differential GPS ($\le 1.0\text{ m}$ precision).

### 3.4 Proposed Soil Physical and Biological Protocols
To evaluate mechanistic degradation, candidate metrics should be analyzed following standard laboratory protocols:
1. **Soil Compaction:** Cone penetrometer resistance (MPa) measured at 0–10 cm and 10–20 cm depth.
2. **Infiltration Rate:** Steady-state infiltration rate (mm/h) via double-ring infiltrometer (*ASTM D3385*).
3. **Aggregate Stability:** Wet-sieving method determining % water-stable aggregates ($> 0.25\text{ mm}$) and Mean Weight Diameter (*Page et al., 1982*).
4. **Soil Moisture Constants:** Pressure-plate extraction determining Field Capacity (-33 kPa) and Permanent Wilting Point (-1500 kPa).
5. **Microbial Biomass Carbon & Nitrogen (MBC, MBN):** Chloroform fumigation-extraction (*Vance et al., 1987*).
6. **Soil Enzyme Activities:** Dehydrogenase activity via TTC reduction (*Casida et al., 1964*); acid and alkaline phosphatase via $p$-nitrophenyl phosphate (*Tabatabai, 1994*).
7. **Mycorrhizal Colonization:** Trypan blue root clearing and gridline intersect quantification of AMF colonization (*Phillips & Hayman, 1970*).

### 3.5 Proposed Faunal & Pollinator Survey Protocol
To measure higher-trophic recovery, timed Pollard walk transects ($200\text{ m} \times 5\text{ m}$, 30 min duration) should be established adjacent to each quadrat to record butterfly species richness, individual abundance, and the ratio of forest-specialist to open-weed-generalist taxa.

### 3.6 Proposed Statistical Model Formulation
Upon completion of the $N=75$ field campaign, data should be analyzed using Linear Mixed-Effects Models:

$$Y_{ijkl} = \mu + \text{MiningType}_i + \text{Season}_j + (\text{MiningType} \times \text{Season})_{ij} + u_{\text{Site}(i)k} + \epsilon_{ijkl}$$

where $\text{MiningType}$ and $\text{Season}$ are fixed effects, $(\text{MiningType} \times \text{Season})$ tests for divergent phenological pathways, and $u_{\text{Site}(i)k} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2_{\text{site}})$ is the random intercept for independent mine/forest site blocks. Standardized Log Response Ratios relative to reference forest ($\text{RR} = \ln[\bar{X}_{\text{mine}} / \bar{X}_{\text{ref}}]$) should be computed across all functional indicators.

## Conclusion

The current dataset supports a cautious comparison of seasonal soil and understory conditions between the sampled underground and opencast mining quadrats. Underground quadrats generally had higher soil organic carbon, total nitrogen, seasonal nutrient values, and dry- and winter-season vegetation persistence. Opencast quadrats showed greater variability, including both persistent woody vegetation and highly seasonal herbaceous cover. These patterns are consistent with different disturbance and recovery states, but they should not be interpreted as causal effects of mining method alone because the current design lacks independently sampled reference sites and broader site replication. The proposed restoration framework identifies the measurements required to test these mechanisms in a future study.
