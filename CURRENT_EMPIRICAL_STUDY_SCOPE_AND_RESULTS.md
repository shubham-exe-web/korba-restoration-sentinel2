### 3.2 Seasonal Macro- and Micro-Nutrient Dynamics
Three-season laboratory measurements across Summer, Monsoon, and Winter document significant seasonal fluctuations driven by monsoon leaching and post-monsoon organic matter mineralization:

- **Soil Moisture:** Surges from $2.8–7.8\%$ in dry Summer to $11.2–25.6\%$ in Monsoon, declining to $5.8–14.5\%$ in Winter.
- **Available Nitrogen ($\text{kg/ha}$):** Lowest in dry Summer ($93.5–233.8\text{ kg/ha}$); peaks during Monsoon ($134.2–335.5\text{ kg/ha}$); intermediate in Winter ($112.2–280.5\text{ kg/ha}$).
- **Available Phosphorus ($\text{kg/ha}$):** Ranges from $6.1–23.0\text{ kg/ha}$ in Summer to $9.4–35.0\text{ kg/ha}$ in Monsoon.
- **DTPA-Extractable Micronutrients ($\text{mg/kg}$):** Iron ($15.6–52.5\text{ mg/kg}$), Manganese ($13.1–41.5\text{ mg/kg}$), Copper ($1.41–4.02\text{ mg/kg}$), Zinc ($0.39–1.43\text{ mg/kg}$), and Boron ($0.26–0.79\text{ mg/kg}$).

## 4. Seasonal Flora and Phenological Observations

Across the three seasons, **42 plant species** across 24 angiosperm families and 1 fern family were recorded in the study area:
- **Summer (Pre-monsoon, March–May):** 28 species recorded (23 trees, 5 shrubs, 0 annual herbs active in open spoil). Deciduous trees shed leaves brief-deciduous or flush bronze foliage; shrubs like *Carissa* bloom.
- **Southwest Monsoon (June–September):** 42 species recorded (100% active foliar growth across all strata). Massive germination of annual forbs (*Parthenium hysterophorus*, *Senna tora*) and perennial grasses (*Cynodon dactylon*, *Heteropogon contortus*).
- **Winter (Post-monsoon, October–February):** 36 species recorded (senescence of ephemeral monsoon weeds; persistence of evergreen and semi-evergreen woody trees and shrubs).

## 7. Empirical Contrast Between OC-Q4 and OC-Q5

Within the opencast group, the empirical data reveal two strikingly different ecological conditions:

1. **Quadrat OC-Q4 (Persistent Woody Thicket):**
   - Directly measured five in-plot woody stems of *Holarrhena pubescens* (GBH 40–110 cm; height 4.3–8.3 m).
   - Maintained high Summer NDVI ($0.4159$) and high Winter NDVI ($0.6703$).
   - Winter-to-Monsoon ratio of $1.0055$, demonstrating persistent foliar greenness through the dry-down period.
2. **Quadrat OC-Q5 (Seasonally Unstable Weed Flush):**
   - Dominated by open disturbed ground with high surface coarse fragments and anthropogenic debris.
   - Lowest Summer NDVI in the opencast series ($0.2137$).
   - Sharp surge during Monsoon to $0.4992$, followed by rapid post-monsoon decline to $0.4696$ in Winter and senescent dieback in Summer.
   - Highest seasonal amplitude ($\Delta\text{NDVI} = 0.2854$) across all 10 quadrats.
   - Field photographs and floristic inventories associate this flush with annual ruderal species (*Parthenium hysterophorus*, *Senna tora*, *Euphorbia hirta*).

## 8. Methodological Limitations of the Current Study

To maintain strict scientific integrity, the following limitations must be stated:
1. **Pilot Sample Size ($n=5$ per group):** Ten quadrats across two mining categories are sufficient for exploratory characterization, but insufficient for broad landscape extrapolation.
2. **Absence of Undisturbed Reference Sites:** The current dataset compares two types of mined land, but cannot quantify absolute ecological recovery relative to native climax forest.
3. **Spatial Clustering:** Opencast quadrats were situated within the Gevra/Dipka complex, and underground quadrats within the Banki/Surakachhar lease areas; true site-level replication across independent mining blocks is required to treat site as a random effect.
4. **Snapshot Satellite Imagery:** Six cloud-screened Sentinel-2 dates provide seasonal snapshots, but do not constitute a continuous daily/weekly time series capable of modeling phenological transitions (SOS, EOS) with mathematical curve fitting.
